Foundation Design Platform

CE 402 Geotechnical Engineering · shallow foundations to IS 6403:1981 / IS 1904:1986 · SI units (kN, kPa, m, mm). Runs entirely in your browser: nothing to install, no data leaves your computer.

1 · Soil profile

Enter one borehole. Layers must run from the ground surface (0 m) without gaps. Only the columns your analysis needs have to be filled (SPT → N; bearing → c′, φ′ or cu; settlement → Es or qc/N for sand, e0, Cc, Cr, cv for clay). Type SPT results as depth:N separated by commas, e.g. 1.5:13, 3.0:16; put ! in front of a result you reject (e.g. !9.0:22); N ≥ 100 is treated as refusal.

Measured water table = at the time of the SPT (used to correct the field N). Design water table = the one used for stresses, bearing capacity and settlement. Leave blank for no water table.

2 · Parameter tools

2a · SPT correction (IS 2131) and φ′ from N

Uses the SPT values and the water table typed in tab 1.

2b · Direct shear: c′ and φ′ by least squares

One line per test: normal stress σn (kPa) then peak shear stress τf (kPa).

2c · Unconsolidated-undrained triaxial: cu

Deviator stress at failure (σ1 − σ3)f for each specimen, kPa. cu is HALF of it.

2d · Oedometer: Cc, Cr, σ′c

One line per increment, in the order tested: pressure, void ratio, and L (loading) or U (unloading).

3 · Bearing capacity (IS 6403:1981)

Uses the soil profile (tab 1) for the overburden pressure, unit weight and the design water table. Strength parameters can be copied from the layer below the base or typed in.

4 · Stress increase with depth (Boussinesq)

Uniform pressure on a rectangle. For a strip enter L at least 10 B (the formulas need a finite L). Depth z is measured below the loaded level (foundation base).

Optional: other loaded rectangles and an evaluation point in the same coordinates (superposition). One rectangle per line: x1 x2 y1 y2 q (m, m, m, m, kPa).

5 · Settlement

Granular layers: Schmertmann et al. (1978) strain-influence method. Clay layers: one-dimensional consolidation in sublayers ≤ 0.5 m (normally or over-consolidated, from OCR in tab 1). Soil data come from tab 1.

Optional neighbouring loaded rectangles for the Boussinesq option, in coordinates with the footing centred at (0, 0): x1 x2 y1 y2 q.

About, assumptions and sources

  • Bearing capacity: IS 6403:1981 cl. 5.1.2: qnu = c Nc sc dc ic + q (Nq − 1) sq dq iq + ½ B γ Nγ sγ dγ iγ W′. Nq = eπ tanφ tan²(45° + φ/2), Nc = (Nq − 1) cot φ, Nγ = 2 (Nq + 1) tan φ. W′ = 0.5 for water table at or above the base, 1 at depth Df + B or more, linear between. Local shear: tan φ′ = 0.67 tan φ, c′ = 2c/3, interpolated by void ratio (0.55 to 0.75) or Dr (70 to 20 %).
  • Net safe bearing capacity = qnu / FS, where qnu is the NET ultimate capacity. Compare with the NET applied pressure (load + footing − replaced soil).
  • SPT: IS 2131: overburden factor CN = 0.77 log10(2000/σ′v) (σ′v in kPa, CN ≤ 2) then dilatancy N = 15 + ½ (N′ − 15) for fine or silty sand below the water table with N′ > 15. φ′ from N: Peck, Hanson & Thornburn (1974) as fitted by Wolff (1989). Correlations are indicative; use laboratory values when you have them.
  • Stress increase: Boussinesq (1885) / Newmark (1935) corner influence factor; centre = 4 sub-rectangles; arbitrary points and several areas by superposition; 2:1 spread (Terzaghi & Peck).
  • Settlement: Schmertmann, Hartman & Brown (1978); Terzaghi (1943) 1-D consolidation; Terzaghi time factor and the series solution for U.
  • Limits: IS 1904:1986 Table 1 and cl. 16.3.5 are the basis of the settlement limits used in the design report, not applied automatically here.
  • Not modelled: capillary rise, secondary compression, seismic and liquefaction effects, bearing capacity of layered soil (punching), pile design. These need separate checks by the designer.
  • Every equation in src/engine.js carries a SRC: comment with its source. Textbook equation numbers differ between editions and are not quoted.

Browser tests, benchmark tests and an independent Python cross-check are in the repository (see README).

Checks and design decisions remain the responsibility of the engineer. This tool shows every factor and every step so that each number can be traced and verified by hand.
